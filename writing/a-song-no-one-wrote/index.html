无人创作的曲子

A Song No One Wrote

A Song No One Wrote

无人创作的曲子

8 min read约 6 分钟First published on WeChat ↗首发于微信公众号 ↗

她人生中感受到的最大的谎言, 不是半路辍学的年轻创业者侃侃而谈某个猴子NFT多么可以被称之为"真正的艺术"; 不是假惺惺的男人女人喋喋不休有头无尾的甜言蜜语; 不是爸爸半醉跪坐在空荡荡的画布前, 一手Malbec一手调色盘, 慢悠悠地说 "不喝酒哪来的灵感". 她人生中感受到的最大的谎言, 是当她得知自己最近爱的那首歌, 不过只是AI合成的工艺品. 她当时刚落地首都机场, 在行李提取处等她的皮箱, 耳机里就放着那首曲子 — Same girl, different wig. 她来得太早, 转盘空荡荡地转, 黑洞洞的出口像是张开的血盆大口, 吐出长长的黑色信子. 她掏出手机确认去酒店的路, 却突然看见这首歌的创作者有了简介 — "现代电子风格的AI音乐家, 始自2021年".

这歌没有歌词, 只是简单的四拍鼓点和低音贝斯轮番交替. 从技术角度来说确实是几行代码就可以编写出来的曲子.可她总觉得这首歌不一样, 可究竟是怎么不一样, 她也说不上来. 老实说, 这不过就是爵士酒廊会放的曲子. 让人想起被厚厚的地毯吸收了大部分的烟草味道, 老式花纹的高背沙发, 窗外昏黄但不黯淡的日暮, 斜斜的黄昏光柱里悬浮的灰尘; 起码得一杯Carbernet Sauvignon下肚, 才能听得来. 她在纽约喝了几杯Carbernet, 就得把这首曲子听了多少遍.

她一个人的时候总是有意地挑一首歌来单曲循环播放, 为的就是给这段独处时光打上一首歌的独特烙印. 就像是做自己人生电影的配音师, 给这个独一无二的片段精心选一段"应景"的配乐. "景"是自己内心世界的反映和对客观世界认识的交融; 是这个地方的客观景象和自己处于这个地方的"目的因"之间的平衡. 所以与其说是"应景", 不若说是在刚进入这个场景时, 对自己在这个场景中期盼的样子, 也就是"应自己在这个场景下的内心世界".

比如夏日傍晚在河边散步, 黑色的天空高远, 水面灯光摇曳, 行人稀少, 景在客观角度上是散漫惬意的, 可你若情场失意, 那听轻快的Bossa Nova显然就不合时宜, 反倒是一些声嘶力竭肝肠寸断的情歌, 或者是自怨自艾的呢喃更能陈情;

比如在阳光明媚的苏豪区逛精品买手店, 店与店之间横跨着建在80度山坡上的100阶台阶, 40度的天气下筋疲力竭, 金属摇滚或是饶舌音乐应该是更能激发你扫荡好物的决心, 可你的目的是观察擦肩而过的各色路人、个性鲜明的买手店店主, 那鼓点鲜明、歌词神乎其神的Deep House可能才是更好的选择;

再或者在冬天清晨的胡同巷子里穿梭找茶馆, 胡同里的老人坐在自家门口打量每一个过往人, 早起的母亲在早点铺买早餐, 小孩子穿着校服垂头丧气的去学校, 市井气息而又欢快的民谣似乎更为合适;可你只是重回故里, 想找到记忆里童年的茶馆, 想从纷扰之中被隔离出来, 静静回忆过去; 那好像听一些在一个月之内就会过时, 但歌词滥俗卖力的流行歌曲, 更能帮你把你从这个所在里偷出来.

一首歌对于一个人, 所承载的关于一段时光的情感和记忆, 是一个非常主观的事情;以上种种, 当然都只是她主观认识的产物. 你大可认为在以上所有场景中, 都要听德沃夏克, 要听雅纳切克, 要听腾格尔, 或者是汪峰; 即使和别人的喜好大相径庭, 自己也说不出来根据和理由, 也没有任何问题. 她一直觉得, 这种无法被逻辑清楚解释的主观认识, 不是造物主施的魔法, 而恰恰是人类拥有主观能动性的证明; 是与生俱来的，同时也是由自身经历所塑造的独特存在，是人类生命独特性和自由意志的一部分。或者是说, 纵使命运庞大而复杂, 如洪流般裹挟着你前进, 她还是有能力决定在这一个短暂而微不足道的人生片段里, 应该放什么样的背景音乐.

为此当她发现这曲子是AI创造出来的时候, 感受到强烈的被背叛的恼怒. 倒也不是说她听歌有什么音乐鉴赏的目的, 非要搞清楚创作者年代社会背景和曲风流派; 毕竟无论这曲子是代码编程出来的, 是巴赫一时兴起创作的, 抑或是天赋异禀的小学生放学后在手机上录下来后上传到抖音的, 都丝毫不会影响她第一次听到这首歌的感受. 她做的和之前所做的也没有什么差别, 只不过是在独身一人来到一个陌生的城市之后, 随机播放歌单里的曲子, 直到有一首曲子让她觉得"应景", 便选它作为这段时光的主旋律. 这首曲子恰好就"应景", 恰好与她在落地纽约后对自己在纽约这几天"期盼中的样子"相吻合, 那不应该就足够了吗?

可她不甚恼怒, 感到一种被侮辱的愤怒. 她以为这是自己精心安排和选择的鼓点, 而这鼓点之所以能莫名地让她百听不厌, 或许是因为曲子的音符涨落和节奏轻缓触发了她一段尘封的记忆, 或许是因为创作者灵魂的些许碎片和她相像, 再或者仅仅是因为在各种复杂变量因素作用之下, 这首曲子以难以名状的原因完美补全了她在纽约的日子里所缺少的东西. 但无论是哪种原因, 她都默许了这样一个假设 — 这曲子的独特性和主观性服务于她的自由意志, 是她选择了这首曲子, 而不是这首曲子选择了她.

但现在看来, 这曲子不过是一串冰冷的0和1; 它被神经网络创造出来, 又被推荐算法送到她的耳边. 它诞生于这个世界, 没有目的因, 也没有结果因. 不是多情的男人为心爱的故人所作, 不是钟爱架子鼓的长发女孩半夜的灵感涌现, 不是年过半百的钢琴家重返故里后的有感而发. 它的创造过程连一秒都不需要, 编写代码的人连它的存在都不知道. 它的名字没有任何意义, 它的抑扬顿挫和高低起伏也没有任何意义. 无论你觉得这曲子多么与众不同, 它都不过是某个机器学习模型习得的产物.

黑色的皮箱终于姗姗来迟, 她拿了箱子, 匆匆走向出口. 海关申报口站着一个穿制服的中年男人, 他举着指示牌, 脸上带着机械职业化的微笑. 不厌其烦, 日复一日, 年复一年的和路过的旅客说, 无申报请走左边, 需申报请走右边. 北京的初春无风, 空气冷得干脆利落, 天空蓝而高远. Same girl, different wig. 她的耳机里还在重复着这首歌.

她站在马路边的台阶上, 看熙熙攘攘的人群被主干道上的出租车和机场大巴慢慢吸纳. 她听到什么, 突然一下子意识到为什么觉得这首歌不一样. 她匆忙掏出手机, 把曲子拉到五秒前. 3分28秒的时候, 有一声女人的叹息. 这叹息很轻, 不留意几乎听不到, 差点就要被鼓点的尾音淹没; 可一旦听到,它便在脑海中挥之不去, 只觉得莫名的沉重, 像是要把积压在心中多年的惆怅一齐吐出. 这声叹息一直都在吗? 这儿为什么要有一声叹息? 她怔怔地想. 可霎那间, 一种巨大的绝望和悲戚摄住了她. 这叹息的存在, 不过是模型中众多变量一起造就的产物; 她以为的出人意料, 以为的独特, 可以轻而易举地被那个巨大的模型中的各种变量所解释, 是AI给她施的障眼法. 叹息在与不在, 究竟为何在, 都无法改变这是一首无人创作的曲子的客观事实, 都没有任何的意义.

车来了, 她把行李放在后备箱, 打开车门坐在司机后面的后座, 据说那是最安全的位置. 她大可以选择坐在副驾上, 可以选择不上车, 可以选择一直待在机场直到夜幕降临, 可以选择把耳边的歌停下. 她看似有着无数的选择, 看似可以决定她命运进程的每一个或大或小的抉择, 可是她和机场的那个男人, 和这世界上的所有人都一样, 只是一块被抛出来的石头. 看似是能决定自己的轨迹, 能穿越不同的时间和空间, 可到头来这轨迹, 不过只是重力作用下的又一条完美的抛物线. 说到底, 她不过也就是Same girl, different wig, 一曲无人创作的曲子罢了.

The biggest lie she ever felt in her life wasn't some young startup founder who had dropped out of college holding forth about how a monkey NFT could be called "real art." It wasn't the endless, half-finished sweet talk of men and women who didn't mean it. It wasn't her father kneeling half drunk in front of an empty canvas, a Malbec in one hand and a palette in the other, saying slowly, "Where would inspiration come from without wine?" The biggest lie she ever felt was finding out that the song she had been loving lately was nothing but a product assembled by AI. She had just landed at Beijing Capital Airport and was waiting at baggage claim for her suitcase, with that track playing in her earbuds: "Same Girl, Different Wig." She was too early. The carousel turned, empty, and the dark opening it came out of was like a gaping, bloody mouth, letting out a long black forked tongue. She took out her phone to check the way to her hotel and suddenly saw that the song's creator now had a bio: "Contemporary electronic AI musician, since 2021."

The track had no lyrics, just a simple four-beat drum and a bass line trading off. Technically speaking, it really was the kind of track a few lines of code could write. But she always felt this song was different, and she couldn't say exactly how. Honestly, it was just the kind of thing a jazz lounge would play. It called up the smell of tobacco mostly swallowed by thick carpet, high-backed sofas in old-fashioned patterns, the dusk outside the window yellowed but not yet dim, dust floating in the slanting beams of evening light. You needed at least one glass of cabernet sauvignon in you before you could really hear it. However many glasses of cabernet she drank in New York, that was how many times she must have listened to it.

Whenever she was alone she deliberately chose one song to play on repeat, to brand that stretch of time alone with a song of its own. Like being the sound designer for the film of her own life, carefully choosing a score that "fit the scene" for that one-of-a-kind sequence. The "scene" was where the reflection of her inner world met her understanding of the world outside, a balance between what a place objectively looked like and her own "final cause" for being there. So rather than fitting the scene, it was really about fitting what she hoped to be in that scene, the moment she stepped into it. In other words, fitting her inner world in that scene.

Say you're walking by the river on a summer evening. The black sky is high and far, lights waver on the water, there are few people around. Objectively the scene is loose and easy, but if you've just been unlucky in love, breezy bossa nova obviously won't do, and some howling, heart-wrenching love song, or a self-pitying murmur, will say it better.

Say you're browsing boutiques in sunny SoHo, with a hundred steps on an eighty-degree slope between one shop and the next, and you're worn out in 40°C heat. Metal or rap should do more to fire up your resolve to sweep up everything good. But if what you're really there for is watching all kinds of passersby brush past, and the shop owners with their strong personalities, then deep house with its clean beat and its mysterious lyrics might be the better choice.

Or say you're winding through the hutongs on a winter morning looking for a teahouse. Old people sit in their doorways sizing up everyone who passes, a mother up early buys breakfast at the breakfast stall, kids in school uniforms trudge off to school, and cheerful folk music with that street-life feeling seems right. But if you've only come back to your old home, wanting to find the teahouse from your childhood memories, wanting to be shut away from all the noise and quietly remember the past, then some pop song that will be out of date within a month, with tacky lyrics trying too hard, seems better at stealing you out of the place you're in.

What a song holds for a person, the feelings and memories of a stretch of time, is a deeply subjective thing; all of the above, of course, was only the product of her own subjective view. You're perfectly free to think that in every one of those scenes you need Dvořák, or Janáček, or Tengger, or Wang Feng. Even if it's nothing like anyone else's taste, and you can't give a reason or a rationale, that's perfectly fine. She had always felt that this kind of subjective sense, which logic can't explain, wasn't magic cast by some creator; it was proof that humans have agency. It was something you're born with and also something your own experience shapes, part of what makes a human life singular, part of free will. Or to put it another way: however huge and complicated fate might be, however it swept you along like a flood, she still had the power to decide what background music should play in this one brief, insignificant passage of her life.

So when she found out the track was made by AI, she felt a sharp, angry sense of betrayal. Not that she listened to music with any connoisseurship in mind, needing to know the creator's era and social background and genre. After all, whether the track had been coded, or dashed off by Bach on a whim, or recorded on a phone by some gifted grade-schooler after school and uploaded to Douyin, none of that changed in the slightest how she felt the first time she heard it. What she had done was no different from what she always did. She had come to a strange city alone and shuffled her playlist until a track felt like it "fit the scene," and chose it as the theme for that stretch of time. This track happened to fit, happened to match what she hoped to be in those days in New York after she landed. Shouldn't that have been enough?

But she was furious, with the anger of someone insulted. She had thought this was a beat she had carefully chosen and arranged, and that the reason it could, somehow, never wear thin for her was perhaps because the rise and fall of its notes and its easy rhythm had touched off some sealed-away memory, or because some fragment of its creator's soul was like hers, or simply because, under the influence of all kinds of complicated variables, for reasons she couldn't name, this track perfectly filled in whatever had been missing from her days in New York. But whatever the reason, she had quietly accepted one assumption: that the song's uniqueness and subjectivity served her free will. That she had chosen the song, not that the song had chosen her.

But now it seemed the track was only a string of cold zeros and ones. It was created by a neural network and delivered to her ears by a recommendation algorithm. It came into this world with no final cause and no consequence. It wasn't written by a soft-hearted man for someone he loved and lost, or by a long-haired girl who loved the drums in a burst of inspiration at midnight, or by a pianist past fifty who had returned to his hometown and felt moved. It took less than a second to make, and the people who wrote the code didn't even know it existed. Its name meant nothing. Its rises and falls, its cadences, meant nothing. However special you thought the track was, it was only something a machine learning model had learned to produce.

Her black suitcase finally arrived, late. She grabbed it and hurried to the exit. At the customs declaration point stood a middle-aged man in uniform, holding up a sign, a mechanical professional smile on his face. Tirelessly, day after day, year after year, he told every passing traveler: nothing to declare, please go left; something to declare, please go right. Early spring in Beijing had no wind, the air crisp and cleanly cold, the sky blue and high and far. "Same Girl, Different Wig." The song was still repeating in her earbuds.

She stood on the steps by the road, watching the bustling crowd slowly absorbed by the taxis and airport buses on the main road. She heard something, and all at once she realized why the song had always felt different. She hurried to take out her phone and dragged the track back five seconds. At 3:28 there was a woman's sigh. The sigh was very faint, almost impossible to hear if you weren't paying attention, nearly drowned by the tail of a drumbeat. But once you heard it, you couldn't get it out of your head. It felt inexplicably heavy, as if it were breathing out years of pent-up sorrow all at once. Had that sigh always been there? Why was there a sigh here? She wondered, dazed. But in that instant, an enormous despair and grief took hold of her. The sigh was only a product of the many variables in the model working together. What she had taken for the unexpected, for the unique, could be easily explained by the variables in that huge model. It was a trick the AI had played on her eyes. Whether the sigh was there or not, and why it was there, couldn't change the objective fact that this was a song no one wrote. None of it meant anything.

Her car came. She put her luggage in the trunk and got into the back seat behind the driver, which was said to be the safest place. She could perfectly well have chosen to sit up front, or not to get in, or to stay at the airport until night fell, or to stop the song in her ears. She seemed to have countless choices, seemed able to make every decision, large or small, that would decide the course of her fate. But she, like that man at the airport, like everyone in this world, was only a stone that had been thrown. It looked as though she could decide her own path, could cross different times and spaces, but in the end that path was only one more perfect parabola under gravity. When it came down to it, she was only "same girl, different wig," a song no one wrote.
