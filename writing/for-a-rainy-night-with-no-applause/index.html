写给一个无人喝彩的雨夜

For a Rainy Night with No Applause

For a Rainy Night with No Applause

写给一个无人喝彩的雨夜

6 min read约 5 分钟Revised March 25, 2023修改于2023年3月25日First published on WeChat ↗首发于微信公众号 ↗

他弹弹烟蒂，残余的星火撞到雨滴"咻"地升起一小滴水雾，灰烬湮灭在眼前的阴雨中。单车溅起的水花沾湿了他的裤脚，偶尔有行人从巨大的伞面下扭过头好奇地看他。他刚刚从一个巨大的、温暖的、冗长的梦中醒来，却已经不知道在这倾斜的屋檐下笔直地站了多久。天空阴沉深不见底，远处是同样无边无际的黑色水面。"远处的水面可能有溺水者在求救，她努力拍打起巨大的水花，声嘶力竭却没有人会听到"，他望着偶尔泛起涟漪的海面，想，"没有人会在意一个人的挣扎，这个世界太过庞大"。

他在的那个梦里，好多事情被不合常理地简单化了。那个世界是小的，单线条的，阶梯式的，但又是甜蜜的；是巴浦洛夫的简单反馈，人们做出规定的反应，就会拿到奖品与补偿。一举一动都有观众。他想必也在那个梦里做出了一些不同寻常、反其道而行之的事情，但举动只是噱头，获得关注才是根本目的。不过他后来摸清门路，想要在聚光灯下得到鼓掌喝彩，最好遵守规则，在既定的路上越走越远。于是他沿着这条路一步步向前走，但同伴越来越少，围观的人也越来越少。掌声还在，却也越来越稀远。

"我是什么时候从梦中醒来的呢？"

雨停了，他最后一根烟也抽完了。他究竟从什么时候开始抽烟，他不记得。"我醒过来，是因为无人喝彩，还是因为我厌倦了这个梦？"

天空更加灰暗，水天交际也更加模糊，升起的月亮点燃黑色的水面。"梦里的海倒是和这里很相似"，他想。幸好，海总是能给他安慰。

但不能总是在这里呆呆地看海。他离开这个巨大城市里小小的挡雨屋檐，朝城市深处走去。这里夜晚街道的灯是冰冷的白色，梦里傍晚的街道永远被昏暗温暖的黄色笼罩。他想必是个好看的人，梦醒时分的恍然又给他增添了几分吟游诗人的倜傥，时不时会有路过的行人侧过脸看他。但他全然不知，或是全然不关心；那些人只是他夜行的过客，没有人会那么碰巧的和他一直在这条路上走下去。毕竟说到底，他是这个城市里再模糊不过的一个人，他的特征和过去，再特殊，也会和至少千百个其他人一同被分享。在这个资本主义的庞大机器里，他没那么特别，也没那么不可替代。他在那场甜蜜的梦里历经千山万水做到的，是这里千百个人，甚至是他们父母梦境开始的起点。这里的人不会因为他的存在而鼓掌喝彩，也鲜少会因为他的离去而痛苦心碎。

天彻底地黑了下来，夜幕覆盖了这座城市。他在十字路口的剧院停下，演出结束的舞者披着厚重的外套走出侧门。末尾的女孩子很是漂亮，纤细的脖子上顶着高傲小巧的头颅。他越过人群定定的看着她，她的一举一动都那么迷人。排着队的疯狂的追求者和观众为她送上鲜花，她美丽的脸绽出一朵笑，但也掩盖不住那一丝疲惫和厌烦。

"在她的生命里，她始终都是那个表演者吗？如果观众都消失，她还会继续跳舞吗？"他迫不及待地想去问她，却又不知道该如何解释这个没由来的问题。"她八成会用些官方的回答来应付吧，比如什么对于舞蹈的追求是纯粹无杂质的"，他想，"但如果是她在那片海域溺水和挣扎，是不是依旧也没有人会听见呢？"

这个想法刚出现他就笑自己，多古怪邪恶的念头，她明明就是这个街区最耀眼幸福的人。他又看了一眼女孩漂亮的脸，转身走掉。

他漫无目的地行走，偶尔向街边忘记拉上窗帘的窗户瞥几眼。这些房间大多有着巨大的落地窗，和与之相称的巨大的电视屏幕。这里住的八成是情侣或新婚夫妻，可能还会再有一只小猫或小狗。下过雨后的深夜确实适合和爱的人窝在家里看些什么 — 没营养的肥皂剧，离奇古怪的纪录片，或者是年久失修的老电影。他在梦中和这个世界遇到的情侣都出奇的相似，他们都愿意在一起做无聊的事。"爱情是不是能给无聊的事情赋予新的意义呢？和不喜欢的人做多么有趣的事情都还是不自在吧"，他想，"这样看来，爱情岂不是为打发光阴正名了？毕竟在温暖的小窝里，就不需要再急急忙忙地去追求什么真理了。"

他看到了旁边窗户里胖胖的柴犬的身影，脚步停了下来。在他刚醒过来的梦里，也有一只这样胖嘟嘟的小狗，她永远都会那么开心地跑向他，摇尾巴。她也有坏脾气的时候，那个时候任他怎么叫她都不应，只会蜷成小小的一团把头埋在尾巴里，偶尔动动小耳朵。但即使她生闷气，也总是会在自己玩的忘乎所以时回头看看他在不在身后，如果离远了就停下来等他。"她爱的是我本身，所以本就不期待我去成为什么，不在意我做出什么成就。不会因为我懒惰而失望，不会因为我贫穷而想要逃跑。我都不需要有多么了不起，多么特别，就可以赢得她无条件的爱和忠诚。"他想，"可当时是我自己对这种真挚的爱感到厌倦，为什么现在又回忆起它的温暖了呢？"

他低下头，回忆突然涌向他，他不得不蹲下去。

地上红砖的缝隙里有小蚂蚁在跑动，他想起在那个梦里，在他小时候，他最喜欢花一个一个炎热的午后看蚂蚁搬运饼干碎屑。"如果我还在梦里，我会更快乐吗？而快乐又是我应该追求的吗？"他问自己。"如果一个人永远呆在梦里，那他是足够幸运，还是倒霉至极？"

他站起身，有点发晕，揉揉太阳穴。周围一个人也没有，他想大喊，想号啕大哭，想奋力奔跑，可是末了，他什么也没有做。只是紧紧地闭上眼，静静等待无边际的孤独一点点吞没他。没有发出一点声音。

He flicked his cigarette. The last sparks struck a raindrop and rose with a hiss in a tiny puff of mist, and the ash dissolved into the drizzle in front of him. Water thrown up by passing bikes had soaked the hems of his trousers, and now and then someone under an enormous umbrella turned to look at him with curiosity. He had only just woken from a huge, warm, endless dream, and yet he no longer knew how long he had been standing straight under this slanting eave. The sky was overcast and bottomless, and in the distance lay black water just as boundless. "Out there on the water someone might be drowning, crying for help," he thought, looking at the sea and its occasional ripples. "She's beating the water into great splashes, screaming herself hoarse, and no one will hear. No one cares about one person's struggle. The world is far too big."

In the dream he had been in, many things were simplified in a way that made no sense. That world was small, single-lined, built like a staircase, and yet it was sweet. It ran on Pavlov's simple feedback: make the prescribed response and you got the prize, the reward. Every move had an audience. He must have done some unusual, contrary things in that dream too, but the acts were only stunts; getting attention was the real point. Later, though, he worked out how things went. If you wanted applause and cheering in the spotlight, it was best to follow the rules and go farther and farther down the road already laid. So he walked that road step by step, but his companions grew fewer, and so did the people watching. The applause was still there, but it grew sparser, and farther off.

"When did I wake up from the dream?"

The rain stopped, and so did his last cigarette. When had he started smoking? He couldn't remember. "Did I wake up because no one was clapping, or because I got tired of the dream?"

The sky grew greyer, the line between water and sky blurrier, and the rising moon set the black water alight. "The sea in the dream was a lot like this one," he thought. Luckily, the sea could always comfort him.

But he couldn't stand here staring at the sea forever. He left the little shelter of that eave in this enormous city and walked deeper into it. Here the streetlights at night were a cold white; in the dream the evening streets were always bathed in a dim, warm yellow. He must have been good-looking, and the daze of having just woken gave him a touch of the wandering poet's dash, so now and then a passerby turned to look at him. But he had no idea, or didn't care at all. Those people were only strangers passing on his night walk; no one would happen to keep walking this road with him all the way. After all, when it came down to it, he was about as indistinct as a person in this city can be. His features and his past, however particular, were shared with at least a thousand others. In the great machine of capitalism, he wasn't that special, and he wasn't that irreplaceable. What he had achieved in that sweet dream after crossing a thousand mountains and rivers was the starting point for a thousand people here, even the place their parents' dreams began. The people here would not clap and cheer because he existed, and they would rarely be heartbroken if he left.

It was fully dark now; night had covered the city. He stopped at the theater at the crossroads. Dancers finished for the evening were coming out the side door, heavy coats over their shoulders. The girl at the end was very beautiful, a small, proud head on a slender neck. He looked past the crowd and watched her steadily; everything she did was captivating. Frenzied admirers and audience members waited in line to give her flowers, and her beautiful face broke into a smile that couldn't quite hide a trace of tiredness and impatience.

"In her own life, is she always the performer? If the audience disappeared, would she keep dancing?" He was dying to go and ask her, but he didn't know how to explain a question that came from nowhere. "She'd probably fob me off with some official answer, about how her love of dance is pure and unmixed," he thought. "But if she were the one drowning and struggling out in that water, would no one hear her either?"

The moment the thought appeared, he laughed at himself. What a strange, wicked idea. She was obviously the most radiant, happiest person on the block. He looked at the girl's beautiful face once more, then turned and walked away.

He walked without aim, glancing now and then into windows where someone had forgotten to draw the curtains. Most of the rooms had huge floor-to-ceiling windows and huge television screens to match. Couples lived here, most likely, or newlyweds, maybe with a cat or a dog. Late at night after rain is the right time to curl up at home with someone you love and watch something: a trashy soap opera, a bizarre documentary, an old movie in poor repair. The couples he had met in the dream and in this world were remarkably alike. They were all willing to do boring things together. "Can love give boring things a new meaning? Doing even the most interesting thing with someone you don't like is still uncomfortable," he thought. "Seen that way, doesn't love justify killing time? After all, in a warm little nest, there's no need to rush off chasing some truth."

He saw the shape of a fat shiba inu in the next window and stopped. In the dream he had just woken from, there had been a chubby little dog like that too. She always ran to him so happily, wagging her tail. She had her bad moods too, when no matter how he called her she wouldn't come, only curled up in a small ball with her head buried in her tail, twitching her little ears now and then. But even when she was sulking, when she was playing and had forgotten everything else, she always looked back to check he was still behind her, and if he fell too far behind she stopped and waited. "She loved me for myself. She never expected me to become anything, didn't care what I achieved. She wouldn't be disappointed because I was lazy, wouldn't want to run away because I was poor. I didn't have to be great or special at all to win her unconditional love and loyalty," he thought. "But back then I was the one who got tired of a love that true. So why do I remember how warm it was now?"

He lowered his head. The memories rushed at him all at once, and he had to crouch down.

In the cracks between the red bricks on the ground, little ants were running. He remembered that in the dream, when he was small, his favorite way to spend a hot afternoon, one after another, was watching ants carry off cookie crumbs. "If I were still in the dream, would I be happier? And is happiness what I'm supposed to be after?" he asked himself. "If a person stayed in the dream forever, would he be lucky enough, or as unlucky as can be?"

He stood up, a little dizzy, and rubbed his temples. There was no one anywhere around. He wanted to shout, to sob, to run as hard as he could, but in the end he did none of it. He only shut his eyes tight and waited quietly for the boundless loneliness to swallow him, bit by bit. Without making a sound.
