写在又一个夏天

Another Summer

写在又一个夏天

5 min read约 4 分钟First published on WeChat ↗首发于微信公众号 ↗

好几次想提笔写自己, 却总是兜兜转转写成别人的故事. 人们生来就被分发各种各样的面具; 戴着面具生活, 戴着面具拼搏, 也戴着面具创作. 有的人一层又一层地戴上更光鲜的面具，有的人则狼狈地撕扯下它们. 是像让别人看到的新的面具是真实的自己, 还是面具之下血肉模糊的脸孔是真实的自己? 写自己，这困境终究是个“古典式的悖论”.

在纽约生活的第三个夏天, 窗外还是同样的风景. 黑色水面泛起灯火的涟漪, 巨大的像房子一样慢慢飘过的邮轮, 夜晚West side high way水泄不通的鸣笛声, 绚烂的千篇一律的玫瑰色的日落在八点不紧不慢地被水面吞没. 我在时间的年轮中大步向前, 却又好像是大踏步地原地转圈. 学生时代的成长是简单的里程碑式的 — 沿着一条拥挤的单向道向前奔跑, 只需要沿着指向标跑到下一个终点.可在纽约这两年恍然意识到, 单向道只是通向无垠旷野的短暂路径. 站在没有路标和参照物的分岔路口, 笔直的路四散开, 具体在哪个分叉口停下还是转弯, 都再也没有什么必须要遵循的标准和规则.

以前一直钟爱《阿拉伯的劳伦斯》里的一句话 — “Nothing is written”.人生是旷野, 自己有无限的自由去书写无限的可能, 而我做出的每一个选择都自由地决定我未来生命的导向, 都将我指引到不同的方向. 可现在的我却也同样相信, A lot about me has been written. 不是悲观的宿命论, 只是一味的向前看反而会忘记来时的路, 回头看才意识到过去的每一个选择和每一段经历都在或多或少地塑造着现在做出决定的我. 我人生中的多重共线性，大部分是因为未曾考虑“过去”这个变量, 或者说未曾考虑过过去经历所塑造出的我自己.

那从这个意义上, 我好像又在成长. 开始学会珍视当下，活在当下; 开始明白人生不是一场启程接着另一场启程, 不是一场告别接着另一场告别, 而是一场漫长的旅途; 开始允许让自己脆弱, 而不是用遗忘和离开去面对失去. 单向道结束后的人生旅途里的成长究竟该怎么定义呢? 或许正是在于不再执着于外在道路, 在于把眼光完全的放给自己的内心.

很长一段时间我总是苦苦寻求于这个外部的世界,想寻得一个合格的glorified的目标去追寻, 想遇见一个可靠的懂我的人去寄托. 可后来我才逐渐明白, 浪漫主义的自我牺牲令人感动, 被世俗认可的鲜花与掌声也固然甜蜜; 可比起所有, 更重要的好像是搞明白自己作为自己, 是想看什么样的风景, 是会爱上什么样的人, 是会被什么样的时刻感动. 比起做其他人生命中的火花和宏大叙事下的唾沫星子, 我更想自私的做我自己人生电影的唯一主角. 比起被爱, 我更想去爱; 比起被选择的幸运，我更想拥有去选择的自由.

我到底是谁? 我究竟又是为何存在? 从柏拉图到尼采, 所有人都能给出一个解释, 一个思路, 但除了我自己没人能给出答案. 而这才是人生的妙处所在. 我活在这浩瀚历史的这么短暂的一个时刻, 是这短暂时刻的无垠宇宙里这么渺小的一个存在, 却偏偏又幸运至极, 能去思考去感受, 能因此明白自己的独一无二. 而正是因为知道自己生命的短暂和渺小, 我反而更想去琢磨透彻, 我之为我, 究其本质是谁?抛开我的出身, 我的种族文化背景, 我的性别, 我的学历工作; 除去这些附加的社会属性, 我到底是谁? 除去社交场合自己和别人握手寒暄三言两语介绍的自己外, 我到底是谁? 如果我存在于一个完全不同的时空, 我又会是谁? 忒修斯之船缝缝补补, 到底哪里不变, 就我还是我? 我当然可以因为生命的短暂, 因为庞大机器的宏大, 而轻而易举地去屈服并选择某一种特定的人生, 并从此说服自己停止思考和质疑, 可那岂不是对真实自我的放弃, 对命运缴械投降, 岂不是等同于厚颜无耻地默认, 我花几十年度过的人生, 不过是为了给那个真正的我提供一个数据点?

世界复杂多变, 人亦如此. 小学语文课上记了太多反义词, 总是雄赳赳气昂昂地评判, 说此人此事不是这样就是其反面. 可后来才发现, 哪儿有那么多的泾渭分明. 亨伯特亨伯特是为爱打破世俗禁锢的情种, 也是法律意义上遭人唾弃的罪犯. 浮于表面的东西总是千姿百态，因此没必要用非黑即白的刻板观念看待世界. 看世界是非黑即白的, 看自己也就非黑即白, 那就必然会限制住自己做这样那样的事情. 但流于表面的变化之中, 总是有些东西是不变的. 就像纽约这个城市, 复杂又统一, 几百年来早已形成它的品性. 初春的时候在下东城呆了不少日子, 算是纽约最年轻热闹的街区之一. 刷了新漆的防火楼梯, 醉酒的年轻男孩女孩的嬉笑, 吐掉的尼古丁含片, 呼啸而过的敞篷跑车, 车轮碾过的烟蒂, 落在砖缝里腐烂的玫瑰花, Katz's deli的霓虹灯光, 蹩脚的三流脱口秀演员把离婚当笑话. 所有的景色都和我窗外的景色那么不同, 和几百年前的景色也那么不同. 但纽约的独特之处恰恰在于统一所有的矛盾, 在于包容一切的底气.

那我也希望往后的自己无论身在何处，无论身边有何人, 无论是狂风暴雨还是阳光明媚, 都能够坦然地说，我在这世间的体验更加丰富，对自己的了解也更为清晰；而这么庞大的一个世界, It's good to be here，and It's even better to be myself.

I've tried more than once to write about myself, and somehow it always turns into someone else's story. We're handed masks the day we're born. We live in them, fight in them, make our work in them. Some people keep putting on newer, shinier ones; others tear theirs off in a mess. Which is the real you: the new mask you want people to see, or the raw face underneath? Write about yourself and you walk straight into that old paradox.

This is my third summer in New York, and the view from my window hasn't changed. Lights ripple on the black river. Cruise ships the size of apartment blocks slide past. At night the West Side Highway is one long wall of horns, and every evening at eight the same extravagant pink sunset sinks into the water, taking its time. I'm striding forward through the years and somehow also walking in circles. Growing up was simple when I was a student. It came in milestones: you ran down one crowded, one-way road and followed the signs to the next finish line. These two years in New York I've realized that the one-way road only ever leads out into open country. At a fork with no signs and nothing to measure yourself against, the road splits in every direction, and there's no longer any rule about where to stop or where to turn.

I used to love a line from Lawrence of Arabia: "Nothing is written." Life is open country, and I'm free to write anything I like across it; every choice I make sends my life somewhere new. But I've come to believe the opposite as well: a lot about me has already been written. Not in a fatalistic way. It's just that if you only ever look ahead, you forget the road behind you, and only when you turn around do you see how every choice and every experience has shaped the person making decisions now. Most of the multicollinearity in my life comes from leaving one variable out of the model: the past, or the self my past has made.

So maybe I'm growing again, only differently. I'm learning to value the present and to live in it. I'm learning that life isn't a string of departures and goodbyes but one long journey, and to let myself be vulnerable instead of meeting loss by forgetting and leaving. How do you measure growth once the one-way road runs out? Maybe by no longer fixing your eyes on the road at all, and looking inward instead.

For a long time I looked outside myself, hard, for a goal grand enough to chase and for someone steady who understood me, someone to lean on. It took me a while to see that romantic self-sacrifice is moving, and the world's applause is sweet, but neither matters as much as working out who I am on my own terms: what I want to see, who I'll fall for, what kind of moment will undo me. I don't want to be a spark in someone else's life or a speck in somebody's grand narrative. I'd rather, selfishly, be the only lead in the film of my own. I'd rather love than be loved. I'd rather be free to choose than lucky enough to be chosen.

So who am I? Why am I here at all? Everyone from Plato to Nietzsche has a theory, but the answer is mine alone to give, and that's the wonderful part. I'm alive for a sliver of this enormous history, a speck in an endless universe, and yet I'm absurdly lucky: I can think, and feel, and know that I'm one of a kind. And because my life is short and I am small, I want all the more to get to the bottom of it. What am I, really? Take away where I'm from, my race and culture, my gender, my degrees and my job, every social label, and who's left? Not the person I sum up in two sentences while shaking hands at a party, but me. If I'd been born in another time and place, who would I be? The Ship of Theseus gets patched plank by plank; what stays the same, so that it's still the same ship? I could, of course, because life is short and the machine is huge, simply give in, pick one kind of life, and talk myself out of ever questioning anything again. But wouldn't that be giving up on myself, surrendering to fate? Wouldn't it be shamelessly agreeing that the decades I spend living exist only to supply one data point for the real me?

The world is complicated and always changing, and so are people. In grade school we memorized lists of antonyms and went around handing down verdicts: a person, a thing, was either this or its opposite. Only later did I learn how little is that clear. Humbert Humbert is a romantic who broke every taboo for love, and also a criminal the law rightly despises. The surface of things comes in a thousand shapes, so why see the world in black and white? Do that, and you'll see yourself in black and white too, and it will keep you from doing all kinds of things. Still, under all that shifting surface, some things hold. New York is like that, messy and whole at once, its character set over centuries. In early spring I spent a lot of time on the Lower East Side, one of the youngest, loudest neighborhoods in the city. Freshly painted fire escapes. Drunk kids laughing. Nicotine lozenges spat on the sidewalk. Convertibles tearing past, tires grinding over cigarette butts. Roses rotting between the bricks. The neon at Katz's. A third-rate comic turning his divorce into a bit. Nothing like the view from my window, and nothing like that street a few hundred years ago. What makes New York New York is that it holds every contradiction together and has the nerve to take it all in.

So wherever I end up, whoever I'm with, in storms or in sun, I hope I can say without flinching that I've lived more of this world and know myself better than I did; and that in a world this big, it's good to be here, and even better to be myself.
