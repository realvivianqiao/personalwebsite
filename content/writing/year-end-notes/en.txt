I'm not quite done being twenty-three, and 2022 has only a few days left. I always used to treat Lunar New Year as the end of the year, but this year, when in Rome, I'm treating December 31 as an ending and a beginning. One by one, the people around me are leaving New York for somewhere warm to hide from the winter. Work has slowed down. On the E train there are fewer people in suits rushing somewhere, and in their place are tourists happily taking photos in Times Square and at Rockefeller Center. The water outside my window has gone a few shades darker. The huge ships that used to dock all summer are nowhere to be seen; Pier 83 is left empty and bleak, with only the occasional ripple of light on the water. A thin frost has formed on the streets, and the doormen have switched to heavy coats and hide in the space between the front door and the inner one.

I'm still happy sometimes and low sometimes, on top of the world one moment and convinced I need to do more the next. Maybe it's because I have more free time now, but I've started thinking about questions like "who am I" and "where do I come from and where am I going," and I've gone back and reread, all in a rush, the Sartre and Saramago and Milan Kundera and Dazai I'd read before or meant to. As a student, so many of the books and films I took in were just for fun; the ten thousand lives in them seemed to have nothing to do with me. Reading them now, it hit me all at once that my life and other people's lives are only crossings and extensions of the lives in books, or that the person I am at this stage is only a miniature of one side of some character.

Milan Kundera said his characters come from sudden flashes of inspiration in daily life, which he then fleshes out until a person emerges. Like the little flecks of mud Nüwa flicked off to make people. Those flecks are Tereza's tangled struggle between body and soul, between kitsch and grace. They are Yozo's forced smiles and affected frivolity after seeing everything the world has to show. They are the beautiful girl in dark glasses, a contradiction of goodness and rebellion. They are Kazuko realizing, in a single instant, her burning love for Uehara. They are all the beautiful girls in Chinese TV dramas who peak too young and aim at the sky while their hands fall short. They are Cat Power softly singing "The Greatest." They are Tom, who dropped out of a PhD at Cornell to drive a cab in New York, with all his resentment and resignation.

But is it "read history and you'll see clearly; know the past and you'll understand the present"? Or is it that we can never know what to want, because, living only one life, we have nothing to compare it to? How easy empathy is, and how meaningless.

In 2022 I spent most of the year chasing things that mean something: work, the gym, love. And the last little bit chasing things that don't mean much: alcohol, romance, paintings, sunsets, and random city walks.

In high school I used to study at the Harbin Institute of Technology campus. Once I opened a notebook some student had left behind, and inside was a muttered complaint: "My ordinary life, my ordinary love." At the time I couldn't understand it at all. I always felt that love and ordinary should be opposites; that even if a life was ordinary, the moment love arrived it would make it extraordinary. But later I slowly came to see that, even now, two people aren't always together because of love. It might begin with worldly calculation, with loneliness, with something unhealthy in the mind, with parents' earnest advice in your ear, with dependence and company, rather than with one startled glance across a surging crowd.

People say falling in love is a process of disenchantment, but to me it seems more like enchantment. Company and reliance are a nutrient that slowly makes a relationship that may not have been quite right, or was even fragile, start to feel normal. They are also a pot of warm water: soak in it long enough, and even if it tastes of nothing, it feels all the more wasteful to throw out. "Love, once made public, grows heavy." And isn't the heaviness, the reluctance to throw it out, exactly the pressure of keeping to responsibilities and promises?

With the person I like lately, I went to Central Park and sat on a rock where you can see Central Park Tower, looking at the black lake, the two of us guessing whether a giant sea monster might suddenly rise from the water. We bought cheap bagged booze at CVS and drank it as we walked, and every strange thing looked like a portal: an oddly wide clearing in a tangle of trees, a cartoon pig sticker on a traffic light, the window of a one-dollar pizza shop, the stairs from a roof deck up to the top floor, a red EXIT at the end of a black hallway. "Light" is always fun, but it always, without your noticing, becomes "heavy," and once it's heavy it goes flat again. But is romance romantic because it's light, or because it's simply romance itself? Or is lightness a prerequisite for romance, so that the moment you start thinking about worldly ties, you have to start calculating the P&L again, weighing the sunk cost, working out the relationship's rate of return?

The pain of being human isn't only anger at your own inadequacy. More of it may come from the gap between what you want and what is, from the second-guessing and hesitation that come from not being brave enough, from lying awake at night with resentment and curiosity about the road not taken. Wanting freedom and drifting, but afraid of ending up on "the lonely sandbar, cold." Wanting stability and calm, but longing to be carefree and wander rough roads. Wanting romance and the flutter of the heart, but afraid of "lowering myself into the dust." Wanting money and status, but looking down on the vulgar. Wanting art and philosophy, but still having to tend my own little patch of ground in front of a screen and keep serving capitalism. In the end you're stuck in between, swinging left and right like the hand of a pendulum. But at all these forks in the road, who knows what the right choice is, the right permutation? And what does right even mean?

At a moment like this, there's no fleck of mud to tell me the answer. I have to walk every road myself. I can only use the flecks of mud to explain my past; I'll never be able to use them to foresee my future.

"A life between heaven and earth is as brief as a traveler passing through." In the new year, I still want to live a little freer, a little more carefree.
