Without my noticing, summer has come to an end again. In the north, the turning of the seasons, wherever you are, seems to be a moveable feast: summer is always the high note, and then comes autumn, slowly fading, and lonely winter, and spring, when everything comes back to life. In Beijing I hated those dry autumns and winters so much that I swore I would live somewhere bright and sunny, spring all year round. And now, outside my window, there is thunder and lightning, the autumn wind is shivering, and raindrops splash ripples on the black water. The air gets colder bit by bit, people change into trench coats and serious faces, and the leaves start to fall. The changing of the seasons, the cycle of all things, the shifting of the world, to the world as a whole how normal it is, how trivial, not worth mentioning. And yet looking at everyone here, you can still feel clearly that summer really is over, and that the romance and happiness made legit by its being summer shouldn't, it seems, go on quite so carefree.

People like to give every season a meaning, and then like to find reasons to hold that meaning up. Autumn is "an ancient road, the west wind, a thin horse," pumpkins and maple leaves and orange. Winter is "on a thousand mountains, not a bird in flight," plum blossoms and white snow and the wine alleys late at night. Summer is Calvin Harris's "Summer," dahlias and beaches and slip dresses. People like to give every memory a song, a painting, and then give it the season that goes with it. As if summer should be a summer fling, autumn a parting, winter a remembering, and spring a new beginning again. So that long, long afterward, hearing the song, seeing the painting, even just the season coming round, still seems to bring back the people and things that go with it, and all the broken little pieces of feeling in the memory.

When I was little I often wondered: is life like a play, or is a play like life? Did the people before us make the rules for our lives so we could follow them, or do we feel our own way and give our lives their meaning? Now everyone can jump up and share their life experience, tell other people what they once did and therefore what you should do. As if life really did have airtight rules, and as long as you followed them you would get the success the rules prescribe. Study hard, have a respectable job, be polite and proper, have a polished exterior, have a partner who loves you. If one box doesn't get checked, then there is something missing. But is the meaning of life that one instant of pause, or all the small moments along the way? And how are you supposed to live your life the way you want, without being bound by all the rules and the world's prescriptions?

Without those worldly bindings, maybe only then could life truly be free. You'd truly know what the things you like, the people you love, the things you want to do would look like, instead of glancing down at the world's reference chart, nodding, and telling yourself this is a choice that satisfies, that impresses, that won't disappoint anyone, and that's enough.

The further I go in life, the more I want to let myself make mistakes, let myself do foolish things, even let myself get hurt. To let myself stop clinging to the idea of my life as a sheet of white paper, and see it instead as a brilliant canvas. A few more bold strokes of color, rather than the ruin of something once spotless.

They say autumn in New York will be over soon, and then it's the cold, bare winter again. On the streets there are starting to be girls in leather pants and tall boots, all elegance, and boys in trench coats and leather shoes and scarves, in a hurry. The water by the river is getting cold, and the lush trees are turning bare. But however bleak the season gets, can't life still go on with a little flair?
